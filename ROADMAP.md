# Roadmap

What's planned for vr-space, grouped by priority rather than dates. vr-space is an experimental hobby project, so this is a direction, not a promise. If you'd like to help with something, open an issue or a pull request.

## Now

[docs/glasses-checklist.md](docs/glasses-checklist.md) lists everything below that needs the glasses, as one session's worth of steps.

- **Investigate stationary yaw drift.** The screen slowly slides right with prediction on or off and in both frame paths; recentering does not stop it. With the glasses resting on a stable surface, raw fusion yaw changed from −0.731 to −0.741 rad (~0.57°) between HUD observations. Opt-in diagnostic logging now compares calibrated FRD gyro means with fused yaw slope over 10-second device-time windows (see the README). A user-confirmed stationary run measured fused yaw drift of about −0.031°/s (~1.86°/minute), with stable nonzero calibrated gyro means (recorded in the checklist). This is consistent with residual gyro bias. Opt-in `--calibrate-gyro` now measures 10 stationary seconds on each connection and subtracts the measured body-frame offset before fusion (not saved). Synthetic tests cover bias cancellation, preserving real motion and accepting stationary sample noise while rejecting excessive variation. A stationary hardware run completed calibration and reduced absolute yaw slopes from about 0.031°/s to at most 0.00070°/s across four 10-second windows. The user confirmed the visible rightward slide is no longer apparent in the app window. Normal head movement was confirmed after the camera rotation-order fix below; deliberate left/right movement during startup correctly rejected calibration without applying correction. Reconnect in the same process also completed a fresh stationary calibration after the motion-rejected startup; a turn-and-stop comparison found tracking stable with prediction off; 20 ms felt shaky while turning, while 10 ms felt better but indistinguishable from off, with no drift or sudden jumps reported.

- **Finish testing on real glasses.** Runs with XREAL Air glasses confirmed that `--fullscreen` opens on the glasses, the screen is sharp and in the right proportions, recentering with `C` works, the screen stays in place as you turn your head, and the neck model feels natural at its default strength, choosing, resizing and closing a captured window works, glasses plugged in after launch are picked up with the connected HUD shown, saved fullscreen returns to the glasses when they are connected after launch, and `--screen-height`, `--screen-distance` and `--fov` behave as described.

- **Position and level investigation — closed; 3DoF limitation noted.** For the previously observed small stable clockwise offset, normal full recentering substantially improved it, and yaw-only mode now uses the first upright recenter per connection as a roll reference; the user confirmed the line stays level after a tilted recenter and during left/right turns. After reconnect, the user reported both lines tilted anticlockwise from the first upright recenter; a later run showed about 5° clockwise tilt. The Controls and glasses menus now offer a saved Keep horizon level toggle and adjustable saved roll trim; trim does not reliably eliminate the offset across reconnects. The user confirmed the toggle and trim survive restart, and normal recentering returns after switching the toggle off. Gyro calibration now waits 3 seconds after connection with a HUD countdown before measuring. The user confirmed the countdown and successful calibrations, but reported that the roll trim still needed changing on each reconnect; the delay did not resolve repeatability. In a 123 mm rail test, stationary reported roll and pitch each changed about 0.63° between ends and returned to their original rounded readings on return; rail twist/slope versus sensor or environmental effects remains unresolved. XREAL Air does not provide positional tracking to this app, so translation cannot be recovered reliably; the exact cause of the orientation changes was not established. The user chose to close this investigation.

- **Correct combined head rotations.** Shoulder tilt was reported to move the view mostly up/down even after recentering, while nodding and turning worked. Fixed the camera conversion to preserve the fusion’s FRD rotation order; the old conjugated XYZ reconstruction mixed roll into pitch at nonzero headings. A regression checks recentered roll across headings and pitches. Build and 86 tests pass. After restarting, stationary gyro calibration and recentering, the user confirmed shoulder tilt now rolls the view rather than moving it up/down. The user also confirmed nodding and turning still work correctly.

## Next

- **Live command-line controls — implemented.** The installed app now accepts `control` requests over a bounded per-user Unix socket, with live JSON status, menu-equivalent changes, explicit setters, native login status and truthful error/unknown-outcome replies. Independent review and socket lifecycle tests cover competing instances, stale cleanup, unsafe paths and bounded clients. Workspace build, all 116 tests and both binaries’ help pass. Signed installed-app checks passed for primary/second sources, invalid-source preservation, main-display alias resolution, rendered layout, menu state, persistence, one-shot log preservation, disabled HUD, `--ignore-settings`, delayed execution after a client timeout, and quit acknowledgement/cleanup. Glasses-dependent recentering and tracking quality remain unverified in this session; no simulator is included. See the README's live controls section.

- **Glasses simulator — implemented, not a hardware substitute.** Opt-in `--features simulate` and `--simulate-glasses` publish synthetic FRD samples through the existing pose path and the live `control simulate` commands. A simulator launch takes the control-socket lock before opening a window and never probes USB. Default build: 116 tests. Simulator build: 127 tests. Both binaries' `--help` pass. A debug `--ignore-settings` launch accepted pose/move/disconnect/connect, rejected a second launch and `--calibrate-gyro`, left `settings.json` unchanged, and a window capture showed `device: simulated` with yaw 0.698 rad after a 40° pose. No real-glasses pass; see [docs/glasses-simulator-plan.md](docs/glasses-simulator-plan.md) and the README.
- **Finish the macOS app.** `scripts/bundle-macos.sh` now builds `vr-space.app`, which opens from Finder, can open at login, holds its own Screen Recording permission and logs to `~/Library/Logs/vr-space.log`. It has its own icon, Dock and Command–Tab presence, and application, Controls and Window menus (sharing controls with the glasses icon). Application menus, Command–Tab switching and minimizing/restoring through the Dock passed manual checks with the release binary. The installed bundle also passed Command–Tab switching, minimizing/restoring through the Dock and screen/latency control visibility in Controls, launched, captured frames with its own Screen Recording permission, and calibrated the gyro; the user confirmed a normal virtual screen after recentering. The user confirmed Open at Login works after the guided logout/login test. Local updates now have a certificate-required `scripts/bundle-macos.sh --run` path: remember the signing identity, quit the old installed app, install and launch only `~/Applications/vr-space.app`, with no ad-hoc install fallback. A signed update on 2026-11-14 after adding the version to the startup log changed the executable and CodeDirectory hashes while preserving the signing identity and designated requirement. The new installed process immediately received BGRA frames without another approval or permission reset; a rendered-window capture confirmed the desktop and connected HUD. Screen Recording permission retention after a changed executable is verified locally. Still to do: a signed, notarized download (which also needs the libusb LGPL terms to be met, see the README). Deferred while the user applies for an Apple Developer Program account; resume Developer ID signing and notarization once membership is active.
- **Survive unplugged glasses.** Implemented: driver errors clear tracking, release the device and retry every second; new sessions reset calibration and prediction. `scripts/cargo.sh` applies the bundled driver patch locally, with no published fork or upstream PR. Physical XREAL Air unplug/replug passed, including with the window on the glasses display after fixing a wgpu 0.17 surface-reconfiguration panic (pending HUD map callbacks are drained before configuring). Regression tests cover tracking sessions and the GPU panic. Three log-verified guided unplug/replug cycles with prediction explicitly confirmed at 20 ms also passed, including unplugging while turning: the same process survived and tracking resumed without wild jumps after recentering. The user also confirmed the disconnected HUD shows `device: not-found`.

## Later

- **More automated tests.** The scene and the HUD are now drawn offscreen on the GPU in `cargo test` and their pixels checked (the HUD tests found, and the HUD now fixes, a Calibrate button too small for its label at large `--hud-size`). The fullscreen logic that doesn't need a window (noticing fullscreen on the display being captured) is tested too. Still to cover: the window calls themselves (borderless, above the menu bar, on every Space), which need a real window and display.
- **Reduce latency further.** Captured frames are now read by the GPU straight from the capture's IOSurface, with no copy through the CPU (`--copy-frames` brings the copy back for comparison). The HUD and log now measure pose → submit, capture → submit and frame upload time; on a 1920×1080 Mac display, sharing cut frame upload from about 1.8 ms to 0.02 ms and mean capture → submit from about 7.9 ms to 4.2 ms. On the glasses at a user-confirmed 120 Hz, a screenshot with prediction off and shared frames showed 120 FPS, pose → submit mean/p95/max of 0.83/1.37/1.62 ms, capture → submit mean of 4.38 ms and frame upload mean of 0.02 ms. Still to do: measure the rest of motion-to-photon (GPU time and the wait for the display), which needs access to the Metal drawable that wgpu 0.17 doesn't give, or a photodiode. `--predict-ms` (off by default) predicts head movement to hide part of the delay. The user deferred further prediction tuning on 2026-11-14: 20 ms felt shaky and 10 ms offered no clear benefit over off. Keep prediction off; the remaining prediction-specific hardware checks are unverified.
- **Multiple virtual screens.** A single curved screen is done (`--curve`, off by default); the user reported “everything looks great” on the glasses at `--curve 1`. Making the curved screen bigger/smaller and moving it closer/farther through Controls also passed. At `--curve 0.5`, the user also confirmed readable edge text, no seams or kinks, and correct recentering. Curve amount is a personal preference, not a remaining pass/fail check. Curvature can now be changed live under **Controls > Screen > Screen curvature** (Flat, Gentle, Full), with the choice saved between launches; `--curve` overrides it at startup. The user confirmed immediate preset switching with correct checkmarks and restoration of Gentle after quitting and reopening the installed app. Gentle was also confirmed checked in the glasses menu after restart. Launching with `--curve 0` was confirmed to open flat with Flat checked, overriding the saved Gentle choice. Two simultaneous captures are now implemented: **Controls > Second screen** chooses another display or window, placed to the right of the first with shared size, distance and curvature controls. Each keeps its own aspect ratio and frame/import state; closing the second window or choosing Off returns to one centred screen. The second source is session-only. Workspace build and all 93 tests pass (including dual-screen GPU rendering and stale-callback rejection); both binaries pass `--help`, and the ad-hoc-signed release bundle builds and verifies. On 2026-11-14, real-glasses testing with ChatGPT and cmux confirmed simultaneous visibility, independent updates, shared/copied switching, resize/aspect handling without overlap, Off/re-enable with primary recentring, and readable Flat/Gentle/Full curvature with correct recentering and no seams or mirrored text. Switching the second source to a Mac display and back also passed, leaving the first unchanged; shared size/distance controls were confirmed to adjust both together. Closing the temporary second window recentres the primary with Off checked; closing the temporary primary falls back to its last display without affecting the second. Restart was checked via the rendered window and menu state: one screen, Second screen > Off checked, capture working. Autonomous app-window captures on 2026-11-14 confirmed a visible gap during sampled size/distance changes at Flat/Gentle/Full curvature. At a CoreGraphics-reported 120 Hz, shared-frame HUD snapshots measured capture mean/p95 of 2.52/6.87 ms with one source, 3.80/9.47 ms with two and 3.63/9.10 ms after returning to one; upload stayed about 0.02 ms mean. Uncapped render FPS was 421.5/267.6/363.6 respectively. Before/after variation prevents treating this as an isolated two-stream overhead estimate. Continuous stutter and wearer comfort remain unverified; see the checklist. More than two screens, custom layouts and saved second sources are deferred.

## Known limitations

These are by design or out of scope for now:

- **3DoF only.** Rotation is tracked, so turning your head works, but leaning or moving does not.
- **macOS only**, and developed only on Apple Silicon.
- **Supported glasses** depend on [`ar-drivers`](https://github.com/3rl-io/headset-utils) and on which models work with it on macOS.
